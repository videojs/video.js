There's a documentary about Video.js now. That's a sentence I never expected to write.

<YouTubePoster id="Kpka8SYbosg" poster={poster} title="Video.js: The Documentary" />

[Cult Repo](https://www.youtube.com/@cultrepo) makes films about open source projects and the people behind them. *Video.js: The Documentary* premiered on their channel last Thursday. It covers the whole arc from a weekend experiment in 2010 to the ground-up v10 rebuild that brought together the people behind Plyr, Vidstack, Media Chrome, and Mux Player.

It features Sam Potts, Rahim Alwer, Wesley Luyten, Christian Pillsbury, Jon Dahl, and me.

## How it happened

Josiah from Cult Repo reached out in March, the same week we shipped the v10 beta. On our first call, we found that the story already had some shape. Part one being the Flash-to-HTML5 era, with a player built over a weekend in the Santa Cruz hills, Steve Jobs announcing that the iPhone would never run Flash, and Video.js ending up on more sites than we ever imagined. Part two is the rebuild, with the project losing its corporate shepherd and a group of player authors deciding to build the next thing together instead of separately.

Filming happened in July, during a week when the v10 team was together in person at the Mux office in San Francisco. We wrote code upstairs while Cult Repo ran interviews in the basement, then took turns being pulled away for "working at the computer" B-roll, which is harder to do naturally than it sounds. They filmed whiteboard sessions, team meetings, and a day at my house. It was a strange week, but fun. Few open source teams get to see their work through someone else's lens.

Watching the finished film was uncomfortable in the way that watching yourself on screen always is. It was also moving to see parts of everyone's story I hadn't heard before. Rahim said it best when he said it felt like it should have been an hour, because there's so much more of each person's and each project's story to tell.

## Why we supported it

Video.js 10 is a new player under an old name. The name carries sixteen years of trust, but it also carries assumptions that it's big and hard to customize. The best way to change that is to tell people what actually happened and why.

So when Cult Repo offered to make the film, the Video.js team and Mux backed it. We wanted more developers to hear that Video.js has been rebuilt from the ground up, by the people who built most of the players they might be using today. A documentary can reach a different set of people than we normally do.

## Thank you, Cult Repo

Thank you to Josiah and everyone at Cult Repo for taking our story seriously and telling it better than we could have. You found the thread through sixteen years of history, made a group of engineers look pretty ok on camera, and handled a long list of corrections with patience.

And thank you to everyone who's used, contributed to, or argued about Video.js since 2010. This film is about you too.

If you've watched it, [try v10](/docs/) and tell us what you think.
